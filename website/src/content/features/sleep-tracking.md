## Who this is for

Most sleep trackers ask you to wear something to bed. Plenty of people don't own a smartwatch, find it uncomfortable at night, or forget to charge it. Habit Tracker gives you a second option that uses only the phone already on your bedside table.

It suits anyone working on a habit like Sleep 8 Hours or Sleep Early: students trying to stop late-night scrolling before exams, people adjusting to an early office shift, or anyone who suspects their sleep is shorter than they think.

## Method 1: from phone use

You set a bedtime window, for example 11 PM to 7 AM. Inside that window, the time your screen stays off counts as sleep. If you pick the phone up at 2 AM to check the time and put it down within 2 minutes, that glance isn't counted as waking. A 20-minute scroll at 1 AM is.

To do this, the app needs Usage access (Android 9 and up). It uses that permission to read only screen on and off times, never which apps you opened or what you did in them. The calculation happens on the phone and the data doesn't leave it.

The honest limit: if you lie awake with the phone face down, the app can't know. That's why it's an estimate. It works best when your phone stays near you at night and you set a window that matches when you actually try to sleep, not an ideal bedtime you rarely hit.

## Method 2: from Health Connect

If you already wear a Fitbit, a Samsung watch or another tracker, its sleep sessions probably land in Health Connect. Choose that method and Habit Tracker reads your sleep from there, as part of [Health Connect sync](/health-connect). It looks back over the last 7 days each time you open the app.

## Making better sleep a habit

Tracking only helps if you act on it. A few ideas:

- Turn on the bedtime nudge in [reminders](/reminders) so “Time to sleep” arrives at 10:30 PM.
- Put the Sleep widget on your home screen so last night's hours are the first thing you see. See all [home screen widgets](/widgets).
- Pair it with a short [night routine](/blog/night-routine): phone on charge across the room, a few pages of a book, lights out.
