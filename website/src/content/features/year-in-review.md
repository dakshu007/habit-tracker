## Why look back over a whole year

A week tells you whether you're on track. A year tells you who you've become. It's easy to feel like nothing changed when you're in the middle of it, then open Year in Review and find you showed up on far more days than you remembered.

It's also a good antidote to all-or-nothing thinking. Missing a week in August feels huge at the time. On a heatmap of 365 days, it's a thin pale line between long stretches of colour.

It also tells you the truth about what didn't stick. If your morning workout was strong until June and then disappeared, that's not a failure. It's a clue. Something changed in June, a new job, exams, the monsoon, and next year's plan can account for it.

## What's inside

Year in Review pulls together everything you've logged:

- **Active days** and **perfect days**
- **Top habit** (the one you did most) and **most consistent habit** (the one you kept up most reliably)
- **Best month** and **best weekday**
- **Moods** across the year, from your check-ins
- **Distance run** and **best run**, if you use the [GPS run tracker](/gps-run-tracker)
- A **day-by-day heatmap** of the whole year

The heatmap is the part people linger on. It's the [habit calendar](/habit-calendar) zoomed out to twelve months, so you can see your strong seasons, the gaps, and how quickly you came back after each one.

## Turning it into next year's plan

Here's a simple way to use your Year in Review in late December or early January:

**Keep your most consistent habit as it is.** It's working. Don't make it bigger just because it's the new year.

**Look at your best weekday and month.** If you're strongest on Tuesdays, that's the day to start something new. If April was your best month, ask what was different.

**Find your biggest gap on the heatmap.** Write down what was going on then. Plan for it, with rest days or a smaller version of your habits.

**Drop one habit.** If a habit was barely active all year, retire it and free up the space.

**Check your moods against your months.** If your mood was best in the months your walking was most regular, that's a strong reason to protect your walks next year.
