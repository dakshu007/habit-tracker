The best habit tracker app is the one you'll actually open every day, and on Android that depends on what you need. Habit Tracker is our pick for most people who want a friendly, private app with templates and quit tracking, Loop is the best fully free option, and Habitica suits anyone who likes games. Below is an honest comparison of eight apps, with prices as listed at the time of writing.

- **Best overall for most people:** Habit Tracker (our app, so judge our bias for yourself). Illustrated cards, quit mode, no account, no ads.
- **Best fully free:** Loop Habit Tracker. Open source, ad-free, offline.
- **Best for Android and iPhone sync:** Habitify or TickTick.
- **Best for gamers:** Habitica, which turns your tasks into an RPG.
- **Best for coaching and routines:** Fabulous.
- **Best habits plus to-do list:** HabitNow or TickTick.
- Most apps limit the free version by number of habits. Check that number first.

## How we picked

We looked at apps that are on Google Play, actively updated, and built for tracking habits (not just tasks). For each one we read the official website or Google Play listing in November 2026 and noted who it suits, what stands out, where it falls short, and how it charges. Prices change and vary by country, so check the store before you buy.

What we looked for in each app:

- **Fast logging:** can you check off a habit in a couple of seconds, from a widget or notification?
- **Reminders that fit real life:** per-habit times and flexible schedules, not just "every day".
- **An honest free version:** what you can do without paying, and for how long.
- **Your data:** where it's stored, and whether you can back it up or export it.
- **Motivation:** streaks, stats, games or coaching that give you a reason to come back.

**Best for:** people who want a calm, good-looking tracker that respects their privacy, including anyone trying to quit something.

## How to choose a habit tracker app

### Count your habits first

Most free plans are limited by how many habits you can track: 3 on Habitify, 5 on TickTick and Habit Tracker. If you're starting out, that's fine. Three to five habits is a sensible number anyway. Our list of [habits to track](/blog/habits-to-track) can help you pick.

### Decide how much you care about sync

If you use an iPhone and an Android phone, or want a web app, choose Habitify, TickTick or Habitica. If you only use one Android phone and prefer your data to stay on it, Habit Tracker or Loop make more sense.

### Think about privacy and backups

Apps with sync usually store your habits on their servers so they can move them between devices. Offline apps like Loop and Habit Tracker keep them on your phone, which is more private, but it means you should set up a backup. Loop exports to CSV or SQLite, and Habit Tracker can back up automatically to a hidden folder in your own Google Drive.

### Simple or feature-rich?

Loop is the simplest. Habit Tracker sits in the middle: easy to use, with extras like templates, routines and a focus timer when you want them. HabitNow and TickTick add tasks and calendars, and Fabulous adds coaching.

### Match the app to your motivation style

Streaks motivate some people. Others need a game (Habitica), a coach (Fabulous), or a friend. Be honest about what has kept you going before.

### Check how you pay

One-time purchases (HabitNow, Habit Tracker Lifetime, Habitify lifetime) suit people who dislike subscriptions. Monthly plans are better for testing. Whatever you choose, note when any trial ends.

## Tips to actually stick with a daily habit tracker

- Start with one to three habits and make each one small ("read 2 pages", not "read 30 minutes").
- Check in at the same time every day, like right after brushing your teeth at night.
- Put a widget or tile on your home screen so logging takes seconds.
- Don't panic over a missed day. Get back on track the next day.
