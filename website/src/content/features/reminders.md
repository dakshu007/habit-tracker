## The problem with most habit reminders

Reminders are easy to set and easy to ignore. After a week of the same 9 PM ping, your brain learns to swipe it away without reading it. The fix isn't more notifications. It's fewer, better-timed ones that only show up when they matter.

Habit Tracker's reminders follow that idea. The evening nudge stays quiet if you're done. Water breaks stop once you've hit your goal. Everything is optional, and all of it is free.

## Every type of reminder

**Per-habit reminders.** Set a time on any habit. It can fire every day or only on the days the habit is scheduled, so your Tuesday and Thursday yoga class doesn't ping you on Saturday. Tie the time to something that already happens: “after I park the scooter”, “after the 7 PM news”.

**Evening nudge.** One check-in at 8:30 PM by default. If some of today's habits are still open, you get a nudge. If not, nothing. It's a good safety net if you'd rather not set a reminder for each habit.

**Daily nudges.** Three optional ones:
- “Good morning plan” at 7:30 AM, a morning prompt to plan your day.
- “Time to sleep” at 10:30 PM, for anyone working on an earlier bedtime alongside [sleep tracking](/sleep-tracking).
- Water breaks every 1, 2 or 3 hours between 9:00 and 21:00. They stop once your water habit is done.

**Weekly review.** Every Sunday at 7 PM you get a notification that your [weekly review](/weekly-review) story is ready.

**Alarms.** Some habits need more than a polite notification. Habit Tracker can save an alarm to your phone's Clock app, set to every day, weekdays or once. It rings even on silent, like any other alarm.

All of these are local to your phone and work offline, so a weak signal on your commute doesn't stop a reminder from arriving.

## Tips for reminders you won't ignore

- **Remind at the moment of action, not before.** A reading reminder at 10 PM when you're already in bed works better than one at 6 PM.
- **Use fewer reminders.** Let the evening nudge cover most habits and keep individual reminders for one or two that matter most.
- **Save alarms for real commitments.** If your habit is a 6 AM run, an alarm is better than a notification you'll sleep through.
- **Put habits where you'll see them.** A home screen [widget](/widgets) is a reminder that never needs to buzz.
