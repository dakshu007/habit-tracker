## For habits measured in minutes

A lot of good habits are really about time: read for 20 minutes, meditate for 10, study for 45, practise an instrument for 15. Tracking them usually means checking a clock, then remembering to type the number in afterwards. The focus timer joins those two steps. Start it from the habit, focus, and the minutes are logged when it ends.

It's useful for students revising for exams, anyone learning a language, people building a meditation practice, and anyone who wants a distraction-free block of deep work.

## How a session works

Pick a habit and choose a length: 5, 10, 15, 25, 45 or 60 minutes. The default is 25, the classic Pomodoro length, which is long enough to get into something and short enough to start without dread.

Choose a sound if you like:

- **Rain** for a calm, steady background.
- **Ocean** for slow waves.
- **Deep** (brown noise) for a low rumble that covers voices and fans.
- **Static** (white noise) for a brighter sound that masks traffic.
- **Off** if you prefer silence.

While the timer runs, the screen stays on so you can see the time left, and an ongoing notification shows the session. When it ends, a chime plays and the minutes go straight to your habit. If it's a 15-minute meditation habit and you ran a 15-minute session, it's done.

Because the session runs inside Habit Tracker, it also counts toward your streak and stats like any other check-in. A week of 25-minute study sessions shows up on your calendar the same way a week of runs does.

## Tips for better focus sessions

**Start short.** If 25 minutes of studying feels hard right now, run a 10-minute session. Finishing a short session builds more momentum than abandoning a long one. This is the “two-minute rule” idea from habit books, scaled up a bit. Our [procrastination guide](/blog/how-to-stop-procrastinating) has more on getting started.

**Clear the desk.** Put your phone face-up on the table with the timer showing, and everything else away. The screen staying on helps here: the countdown is a visible commitment.

**Stack sessions in a routine.** Put a focus habit inside a [routine](/routines), for example Journal, then Read with a 15-minute session, then Plan tomorrow. One routine, one flow.

**Use the right habit unit.** The timer logs minutes, so set up your habit in minutes. The [habit templates](/habit-templates) for Read, Meditate and Learn Something can all be set to minutes.
