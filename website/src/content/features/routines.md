## Why routines beat a loose list of habits

When habits sit in a list, every one is a separate decision. Should I journal now or after breakfast? Did I drink water yet? Each choice takes a little willpower, and in the morning there isn't much to spare.

A routine removes those decisions. You decide the order once, then just follow it. This is the idea behind habit stacking: link a new habit to one you already do, so finishing one becomes the cue for the next. “After I brush my teeth, I drink a glass of water. After water, I stretch for 5 minutes.” Habit Tracker's routines make that chain something you can actually play.

## How playing a routine feels

Open a routine and it starts on the first habit card, illustration and all. Do the habit, slide to complete, and the next card appears. There's no list to scan and no other habits pulling at you.

When you reach the end, you see a simple summary like “4 of 5 habits done”. If you skipped one because you were short on time, it's still open for later. The goal is to get through most of it on most days, not to be perfect.

Because every slide completes the real habit, your streaks, XP and stats update as you go. The routine is just a faster, calmer way through them.

## Routine ideas to start with

**Morning (10 minutes):** Drink Water, a short stretch, Journal for 3 minutes, plan the day. See our [morning routine guide](/blog/morning-routine) for more ideas.

**Study block:** Screen-free Hour, then Read or Learn Something with a 25-minute [focus timer](/focus-timer) session, then a short walk.

**Wind-down:** Phone on charge, Read 10 pages, Meditate, Sleep Early. Pair it with the bedtime nudge in [reminders](/reminders).

**After work:** Walk, Eat Greens at dinner, Cleaning for 10 minutes, tomorrow's clothes out.

## Tips for routines that last

- **Keep it short.** Three to five habits is plenty. A 12-step routine is a lot to restart after a missed day.
- **Start with an anchor.** Begin the routine with something you already do every day, like making chai.
- **Put the hardest habit early.** Energy and willpower fade, so place the tough one second, after an easy warm-up.
- **Review after a week.** If you keep skipping the same habit, move it, shrink it, or take it out.
