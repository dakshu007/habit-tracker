## Who reports are for

Most of the time, the app's own screens are all you need. Reports are for the moments you want your habits outside the app:

- **Monthly check-ins with yourself.** A one-page PDF at the end of each month makes a good habit journal, especially if you keep them in one folder.
- **Sharing with someone who helps you.** A trainer, a mentor, a study partner or a friend you've asked to keep you honest can read a PDF without installing anything.
- **Spreadsheet people.** If you like making your own charts, the CSV gives you every entry to work with.
- **Keeping records.** Some people like an archive of what they did, month by month, in plain files they control.

## PDF or CSV?

**The PDF** is a one-page A4 summary of the month. It's made for reading: open it, glance at it, done. It's the one to send to someone who just wants to know how your month went.

**The CSV** is every entry for the month, one row at a time. It's made for working with. Open it in Google Sheets or Excel, sort by habit, build a pivot table, or combine several months into one sheet.

Both are created on your phone from the data stored there, then handed to Android's share sheet. Where the file goes next is up to you: save it to your files, attach it to an email, or drop it in a WhatsApp chat. Nothing is uploaded to make the report.

Reports aren't the same as a backup. If you want to move to a new phone or keep a copy you can restore, use [backup and restore](/backup-and-restore) instead.

## Ideas for using your reports

**Make a last-day-of-the-month habit.** Export the PDF, look at it for two minutes and write one line about next month. It pairs well with your free [stats](/stats-and-insights).

**Compare months in a sheet.** Export CSVs for three months and put them side by side. You'll see which habits held up and which faded after week two.

**Send it to your buddy.** If someone checks in on you daily, a monthly PDF gives them the bigger picture.
