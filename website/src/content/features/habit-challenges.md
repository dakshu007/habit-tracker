## A finish line makes it easier to start

“Read more” is a wish. “21 Days of Reading, starting Monday” is a plan with an end date. That's the idea behind challenges: a short, fixed stretch you can say yes to without committing to forever.

The 8 challenges cover the habits people most often want to try:

- **7 days:** Hydration Reset, Screen Detox
- **21 days:** Reading, Calm, Running Start
- **30 days:** Walking Streak, Journaling, Fitness Kickoff

If you're new to habit tracking, start with a 7-day one. Finishing something small builds more confidence than abandoning something big on day 9.

Match the challenge to your month, too. Exam season is a good time for 21 Days of Calm, not the 30-Day Fitness Kickoff. A month with long evenings and fewer deadlines is the moment for a running or walking challenge.

A note on the numbers: 21 or 30 days won't make a habit automatic on their own. In a 2009 study by Lally and colleagues, people took a median of 66 days to reach automaticity, with a wide range. Think of a challenge as a strong start. Our post on [how long it takes to form a habit](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-form-a-habit) goes deeper.

## Why 80% and not 100%

Perfect streaks are fragile. One fever, one late night at the office, and a 100% rule turns into “well, I've failed, might as well stop”. The 80% rule leaves room for real life. On a 30-day walking challenge you can miss 6 days and still earn the trophy, as long as you keep coming back.

When a challenge ends, pick the next one, or add the habit to your daily list and build a [streak](/streaks) the usual way.

## Doing it with a friend

Every challenge has a code in a format like RUN21-261012. Send it to a friend, a sibling or your hostel roommate. When they enter it, you both start the same challenge on the same day, so “how's day 12 going?” means the same thing to both of you.

Pick someone who wants the same thing you do. A friend who's also trying to cut screen time is a better partner for the Screen Detox than your fittest friend is.

Codes don't create accounts or link your data. There's no leaderboard and nobody can see your check-ins. You keep each other going the old-fashioned way: messages. To make that easier, send a [share card](/share-progress) of your streak, or set up a daily WhatsApp check-in with an [accountability buddy](/accountability-buddy).
