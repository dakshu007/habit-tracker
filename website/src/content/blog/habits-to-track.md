The best habits to track are small, specific and tied to something you actually want, like more energy, better sleep, a calmer mind or less time on your phone. Below are 75 habit tracker ideas by category, each with a one-line reason and a starting target small enough to hit on a bad day. Don't try all 75: pick three and track them for a month.

## Key takeaways

- **Start with 3 habits**, not 15.
- **Make every habit measurable**: a number (6 glasses), a time (in bed by 11) or a clear yes/no.
- **Start below what you think you can do.** Raise the target later.
- **Track quit habits as clean days**, not slips.
- **Review once a week** and adjust the target, not the whole habit.

## Health habits to track

1. **Drink water.** Mild dehydration can leave you tired. Start: 6 glasses a day.
2. **Eat a fruit or vegetable with lunch.** It's the easiest meal to upgrade. Start: one serving at lunch.
3. **Take medication or vitamins.** Ends the "did I take it?" doubt. Start: daily, right after breakfast.
4. **Floss.** Brushing misses the gaps between teeth. Start: floss just your front teeth.
5. **Cook at home.** You control oil, salt and cost. Start: 3 home-cooked dinners a week.
6. **Add protein to breakfast.** It keeps you full until lunch. Start: eggs, paneer, dal or curd with breakfast.
7. **Stand up every hour.** Long sitting leaves you stiff. Start: stand and stretch once an hour during work.
8. **Eat slowly.** It's easier to notice when you're full. Start: put your spoon down between bites at dinner.
9. **Wear sunscreen.** It protects your skin every single day. Start: apply it before you leave home.

## Fitness habits to track

10. **Walk.** It's free and needs no gear. Start: 5,000 steps, or a 15-minute walk after dinner.
11. **Work out.** Strength helps posture and energy. Start: 2 sessions of 20 minutes a week.
12. **Do push-ups.** A tiny habit you can do anywhere. Start: 5 push-ups (on your knees is fine).
13. **Stretch.** It loosens you up after a night's sleep. Start: 5 minutes after waking.
14. **Run.** Short runs build up surprisingly fast. Start: 20 minutes of run-walk, 3 times a week.
15. **Take the stairs.** Built-in exercise on a normal day. Start: 3 floors a day.
16. **Do yoga.** It mixes movement and calm. Start: 5 rounds of surya namaskar, 3 mornings a week.
17. **Cycle.** Transport and cardio in one. Start: one short trip by cycle each week.
18. **Hold a plank.** A simple test of core strength. Start: 30 seconds a day.

## Sleep habits to track

For more ideas, see our [night routine guide](/blog/night-routine).

19. **Wake up at the same time.** It steadies your whole day. Start: within 30 minutes of the same time, every day.
20. **Go to bed on time.** You can't wake early if you sleep late. Start: in bed by 11 pm on weeknights.
21. **Keep the phone out of bed.** Scrolling in bed pushes sleep later. Start: phone on the charger across the room.
22. **Log your sleep hours.** Measure before you change. Start: log hours for two weeks, then set a target.
23. **Cut late caffeine.** Caffeine stays in your system for hours. Start: last chai or coffee by 2 pm.
24. **Get morning light.** Daylight early in the day helps set your body clock. Start: 10 minutes outside within an hour of waking.
25. **Wind down.** Your brain needs a runway before sleep. Start: 15 minutes of dim lights and a book.

## Mind and mood habits to track

26. **Meditate.** Short sessions still count. Start: 2 minutes of slow breathing after brushing your teeth.
27. **Journal.** Writing things down clears your head. Start: 3 lines before bed.
28. **Note one good thing.** It trains you to spot what's working. Start: one line a day.
29. **Check in on your mood.** Patterns show up after a few weeks. Start: rate your mood once a day.
30. **Spend time outside.** It resets your attention. Start: 10 minutes outdoors without your phone.
31. **Have a screen-free hour.** It gives your mind a real break. Start: the first hour after waking or the last before bed.
32. **Do something just for fun.** Not everything needs a goal. Start: 15 minutes of a hobby, 3 times a week.
33. **Limit news.** Constant updates add stress. Start: check the news once a day at a set time.
34. **Walk without headphones.** Quiet time lets ideas surface. Start: one silent walk a week.

## Work and money habits to track

43. **Plan tomorrow.** You start the day knowing what matters. Start: write your top 3 tasks before closing the laptop.
44. **Do deep work.** Focused blocks get more done than a scattered day. Start: one 25-minute block with your phone in another room, using a [focus timer](/focus-timer).
45. **Process email in batches.** Constant checking breaks focus. Start: email twice a day, at set times.
46. **Track spending.** Small spends add up unnoticed. Start: log every expense over ₹100.
47. **Save automatically.** Saving first beats saving what's left. Start: a fixed amount moved to savings on payday.
48. **Have a no-spend day.** It shows which spends are habit. Start: one no-spend day a week.
49. **Do the hard task first.** Your energy is highest early. Start: 15 minutes on it before checking messages.
50. **Do a weekly review.** It catches problems before they grow. Start: 20 minutes every Sunday.
51. **Work on a side project.** Small sessions keep it alive. Start: 30 minutes, twice a week.
52. **Pack lunch.** Saves money and usually means better food. Start: 3 days a week.

53. **Make your bed.** One finished task before 8 am. Start: every morning, 1 minute.
54. **Clear the sink before bed.** Mornings start cleaner. Start: sink empty every night.
55. **Do a 10-minute tidy.** Little and often beats a weekend clean-up. Start: set a timer every evening.
56. **Do laundry on a set day.** No more running out of clean clothes. Start: one fixed laundry day a week.

## Bad habits to quit (track them as clean days)

67. **Smoking.** The single biggest health win on this list. Start: track clean days, and talk to a doctor or a quitline for support.
68. **Doomscrolling.** Endless feeds eat your evenings. Start: no social apps before 10 am.
69. **Junk food.** Packaged snacks add up fast. Start: no packaged snacks on weekdays.
70. **Sugary drinks.** An easy swap with a big payoff. Start: replace one cold drink a day with water.
71. **Nail biting.** Awareness is the first step. Start: track bite-free days and keep your hands busy.
72. **Hitting snooze.** It starts the day with a broken promise. Start: put the alarm across the room.
73. **Late-night snacking.** It often affects sleep and digestion. Start: kitchen closes at 9:30 pm.
74. **Drinking alcohol.** Fewer drinks often means better sleep. Start: alcohol-free weekdays. If you feel dependent, speak to a doctor first.
75. **Impulse shopping.** Most urges fade within a day. Start: wait 24 hours before any non-essential purchase.

## How to choose your first 3 habits

### 1. Start from a goal, not the list

Ask what you want more of in three months: energy, focus, money, calm or fitness. Pick habits that serve that one goal.

### 2. Pick one easy win, one core habit and one stretch

The easy win gives you a daily tick. The core habit does the heavy lifting. The stretch is what you're building towards.

### 3. Shrink each habit to its starting target

If a habit takes more than 10 minutes on a bad day, cut it down. Showing up comes first.

### 4. Give each habit a time and a place

"Read more" rarely happens. "Read 10 pages on the metro home" does. Attach each habit to something you already do, like your morning chai. Our guide to [building good habits](/blog/how-to-build-good-habits) explains this in detail.

## How to keep tracking past week two

- **Log straight after doing it.** If you wait until night, you'll forget.
- **Never miss twice.** One missed day is normal. Two in a row starts a new pattern.
- **Use weekly targets where it fits**, like workouts 3 times a week.
- **Review on Sundays.** Shrink any target you missed more than half the time.

Habits take longer than most people expect. A study by Lally and colleagues (2009) found a median of 66 days for a daily habit to feel automatic, ranging from 18 to 254 days. Judge your list after two months, not two weeks. More on this in [how long it takes to form a habit](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-form-a-habit).
