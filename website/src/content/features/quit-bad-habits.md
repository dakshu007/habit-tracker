## Why counting clean days helps

Building a habit gives you something to tick off: a run, a page, a glass of water. Quitting gives you nothing to tick. Success is an absence, and absences are easy to forget by Thursday.

Quit mode makes that absence visible. Every evening you slide once to confirm the day was clean, and the number on the card goes up. After a week, “7 days clean” is a small fact you can point to. After a month, it's something you'd rather not reset over one cigarette at a wedding.

If you want the thinking behind this, our guide on [how to break a bad habit](/blog/how-to-break-a-bad-habit) covers triggers, replacements and what to do after a slip.

## How quit mode tracks your progress

Each quit habit shows four numbers:

- **Days clean:** your current run since the last slip.
- **Best run:** the longest run you've had so far.
- **Slips:** how many times you've tapped “I slipped”.
- **Money saved:** clean days multiplied by your daily cost.

The three [quit templates](/habit-templates) come with sensible defaults. Quit Smoking assumes ₹150 a day and No Junk Food ₹100 a day, but you should change these to your real numbers. If you spend ₹60 on a samosa and cold drink every afternoon, use ₹60. Less Doomscrolling counts days only, because the cost there is your evening, not your wallet.

You can also set up a custom quit habit for anything else: energy drinks, late-night food delivery, impulse shopping. The currency follows your setting, so the money figure makes sense wherever you live.

## Tips for the first two weeks

**Confirm at the same time every night.** Brushing your teeth is a good anchor. Pair it with a [reminder](/reminders) so you don't go to bed without sliding.

**Plan your hard moments.** If you always scroll in bed, charge your phone across the room. If you smoke with chai, change where you drink your chai for a while.

**Treat a slip as data.** Tap “I slipped” honestly. Then look at what happened: the time, the place, who you were with. Your best run stays on screen, and beating it is a better goal than pretending the slip didn't happen.

**Watch the money grow.** Some people move the saved amount into a separate account once a month. Seeing ₹3,000 turn into something you actually want makes day 21 easier than day 3.
